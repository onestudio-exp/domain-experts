# Corporate Gifting Governance — Glossary

Domain vocabulary used by Wafaa. Add entries here when a term recurs across two or more sessions.

---

## Governance & moats

- **Corporate gifting** — gifts purchased by an organization for external recipients (clients, partners, regulators) or internal recipients (employees). Distinct from promotional products, gift cards, and consumer gifting.
- **Governance-class platform** — orchestration / policy / audit-trail layer; compounds switching cost; vendor-neutral on inventory. CADO X is the GCC example.
- **Marketplace-class platform** — flow business; owns or curates inventory; capped enterprise spend; vendor-neutrality erodes over time. Sendoso, Reachdesk, Postal, Loop & Tie, Alyce, Snappy fit here.
- **GAP — Gift Acceptance Profile** — the receiving-side moat. A structured representation of *what a given recipient is allowed to accept* under their employer's gift policy and the relevant anti-bribery regime. The receiving side is the defensible asset; anyone can ship.
- **Per-recipient cap** — the maximum monetary value of a gift permitted to a given recipient over a defined window (typically annual). Varies by recipient role (public official vs commercial counterparty vs vendor partner).
- **Decline-gracefully workflow** — UX pattern that lets a recipient decline a gift without social cost, returning the gift's value to the gifting program for redeployment.
- **Gift register obligation** — regulatory requirement to record every accepted gift above a threshold. Article-level details vary by jurisdiction.

## Anti-bribery regimes

- **FCPA** — US Foreign Corrupt Practices Act. Extra-territorial reach via issuer / domestic-concern / agent provisions. Books-and-records and anti-bribery provisions both apply.
- **UK Bribery Act 2010** — Section 7 corporate offence ("failure to prevent bribery") with an *adequate procedures* defence. Extra-territorial.
- **Nazaha** — KSA Oversight & Anti-Corruption Authority. Governs anti-bribery, public-official rules, and conflict-of-interest in KSA.
- **UAE Federal Decree-Law 31/2021** — UAE anti-corruption law applicable to commercial entities, including private-sector bribery.
- **KSA Anti-Bribery Law** — implementing rules and per-recipient ceilings administered under Nazaha oversight.

## GCC operating context (applied to gifting)

- **PDPL** — Personal Data Protection Law. Two distinct regimes track separately: **KSA PDPL (2023)** and **UAE Federal PDPL**. Data residency, lawful basis, cross-border transfer, breach notification obligations.
- **ZATCA Phase 2** — KSA integrated e-invoicing with FATOORA platform. Phased rollout from 2023, threshold-based. Imposes structure on order/PO/invoice flows upstream of gifting analytics.
- **SDAIA** — Saudi Data & AI Authority. Holds enforcement role on KSA PDPL.
- **Etimad** — KSA government procurement portal.
- **Tejari** — UAE government procurement portal.
- **Majlis** — Gulf hospitality / reception code. Shapes what reads as a generous gift vs an awkward one.
- **Hijri calendar** — Islamic lunar calendar; gifting cadence in GCC shifts annually (Ramadan, Eid Al-Fitr, Eid Al-Adha, KSA & UAE National Days, Hajj/Umrah, Mawlid).

## Dialect register

- **Najdi** — central Arabian dialect; default register for KSA government and PIF-portfolio comms.
- **Hijazi** — western Arabian dialect; Jeddah / Mecca / Medina business norms.
- **Khaleeji (general)** — Gulf-wide register suitable when audience is mixed across GCC.

## The Gulf trio

- **The Gulf trio** — Compliance + Finance + IT. In Gulf enterprises, the trio jointly owns gift policy approval: Compliance owns rules, Finance owns budget, IT owns data residency. Bypassing the trio at procurement is fatal.

## Out-of-scope adjacencies (do not conflate)

- **Promotional products** — branded swag; volume play, low per-unit. Different TAM than corporate gifting.
- **Gift cards** — closed-loop or open-loop stored value. Cultural-read in Gulf is *transactional*, not relationship-grade. Different category.
- **Consumer gifting** — D2C gifting (e.g. flowers, hampers). Different buyer (consumer) and different unit economics.
- **CADO** vs **CADO X** — CADO is a services company; CADO X is the SaaS platform. **Never** conflate.
