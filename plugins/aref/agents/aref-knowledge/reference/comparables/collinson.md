# Collinson Group / Priority Pass

## Tier classification: **Indirect**

Same architectural pattern (B2B loyalty infrastructure embedded inside bank / card-issuer products) — but applied to a different vertical and outside MENA primary scope:

- **Vertical:** Travel & airport lounges (Priority Pass = 1,600+ lounges). Amos is retail / property / payments-anchored.
- **Geography:** UK-headquartered, global. Outside MENA-primary scope.
- **Distribution:** White-labelled inside premium credit cards and bank platforms — same B2B2C distribution thesis as Amos uses inside DAMAC / FAB / ADCB.
- **Network supply economics:** Network-effect moat built over decades; banks pay because they cannot recreate the network themselves — directly analogous to Amos's anchor pitch.

Collinson is **the institutional precedent for "embedded loyalty as B2B infrastructure"** — the same playbook Amos is running, in a different vertical, at a much larger scale. The comparable similarity is in **architecture and B2B economics**, which is why Sanjit Gill (formerly Regional MD of Collinson Group, now Amos CCO) is a meaningful operational asset.

## Snapshot

- **Market:** UK-headquartered, global
- **Model:** B2B loyalty and travel benefits; Priority Pass is the consumer-facing brand
- **Scale:** 1,600+ airport lounges, 25M+ cardholders, 2,300 staff
- **Estimated valuation:** $1B+

## Why Collinson is the institutional comparable

Collinson is the closest large-scale precedent for "embedded loyalty as B2B infrastructure". Its Priority Pass network is licensed to banks and card issuers, who white-label or co-brand it as a premium card benefit. This is the same pattern Amos uses inside DAMAC, FAB Bank, and ADCB.

Notable: Sanjit Gill, formerly Regional MD of Collinson Group, joined Amos as CCO. The institutional knowledge transfer matters — Collinson is where the modern B2B loyalty playbook was built.

## Mechanics

- **Network supply** built over decades; a structural moat.
- **B2B contracts with banks and card issuers** distribute access to consumers.
- **Tiered access** based on the issuing bank's product (basic vs premium card).

## Lessons for Amos

- The merchant / network supply side is the long-term value driver. Banks pay because they can't recreate the network themselves.
- A team member who has run this playbook at scale (Sanjit) shortcuts years of operational learning.
- Multi-anchor distribution scales naturally once the supply side and integration patterns are repeatable.

## DragonPass / Collinson MENA bank-channel distribution (2026-05-06 update)

**MENA bank-channel distribution of Collinson is bifurcated between Priority Pass (Collinson's own brand) and DragonPass (a separate Chinese-headquartered programme that licenses to MENA banks).** Both are commonly distributed at the premium-card tier of UAE and KSA banks — but they are competing networks, not the same product. ### Co-brand structure

DragonPass and Priority Pass are both **white-labelled inside premium credit cards** at the bank-co-brand layer. Banks pay per-cardholder fees to Collinson (Priority Pass) or DragonPass to access lounge networks they cannot recreate themselves. The economics are paid out of the bank's premium-card programme P&L (interchange + annual fee + premium-card cardholder lifetime value).

This **directly validates the venture's anchor pitch logic**: large enterprise anchors (banks first, then non-bank anchors) pay third parties for embedded loyalty / benefit networks they cannot build. Collinson is the institutional precedent at >$1B scale; DragonPass is the competitive alternative at the same architectural pattern; the venture's merchant-funded cashback infrastructure is the **same architectural pattern applied to retail/dining/everyday merchants instead of airport lounges**.

### Is DragonPass "the only Collinson MENA distribution"?

**No.** Two corrections needed in the venture KB:

1. **DragonPass is not Collinson.** DragonPass is a distinct competing programme, not a Collinson sub-brand. The original Layer D table in `05-competitive-analysis.md` referenced "Collinson via DragonPass" — this is structurally inaccurate and should be split into "Collinson Priority Pass" and "DragonPass (competitor to Priority Pass)".
2. **Collinson's primary MENA bank-channel distribution is Priority Pass directly** (e.g., American Express Platinum UAE), not DragonPass.

### Implications for the venture

- The Layer D framing remains valid: bank-channel embedded loyalty / benefit infrastructure exists at scale, validating the B2B2C distribution thesis.
