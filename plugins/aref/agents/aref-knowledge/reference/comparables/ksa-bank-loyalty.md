# KSA Bank Loyalty Programmes

## Tier classification: **Indirect**

Same loyalty outcome (consumer earns value on retail spend) via a structurally different model from this venture's merchant-funded B2B2C cashback infrastructure:

- **Funding source:** Bank P&L (interchange-on-Mada+international + acquirer share + bank-issued reward liability) — NOT merchant MDR-share.
- **Distribution:** Bank's own app + bank's own credit-card products. Closed to that bank's customers.
- **Anchor licensability:** **None of the programmes profiled here are licensable to a third-party enterprise anchor.** They are bank-proprietary.

KSA market context: per the Saudi Arabia Loyalty Business Report 2026, the KSA loyalty market is sized at $712.3M in 2025 growing to $1.27B by 2030 [`[VERIFIED]` — GlobeNewswire 2026-02-06]. The named anchor platforms in that report are stc Qitaf, Al Rajhi Bank Card Loyalty, Saudia AlFursan, and stc Pay — confirming that **bank loyalty in KSA is dominated by closed bank-proprietary programmes**, not by B2B2C licensable infrastructure.

## Cross-cutting observations

1. **No KSA bank operates a licensable B2B2C cashback infrastructure.** Every programme above is a closed-loop bank-proprietary instrument funded by interchange + bank P&L.
2. **The dominant external co-brand pattern is the airline tie-up** (Saudia AlFursan partners with Mokafaa, SNB, Riyad Bank, Alinma). This validates the "loyalty network outside the bank's own merchant scope" thesis — but the airline owns the licensable layer, not the bank.
3. **Cashback in KSA bank cards is structured as direct merchant-category rebate** (e.g., Riyad 10% on eligible categories with min spend SAR 2,500/month), not as tradeable points. This sets a consumer expectation of ~10% headline cashback in named categories, which is **higher** than the venture's likely consumer-facing rate. Anchor positioning needs to account for the headline-rate gap.
4. **Mokafaa accepts at 170+ merchants** — interesting parallel to Amos's "~170 direct merchants" footprint, but Mokafaa's network is a redemption acceptance footprint inside Al Rajhi's relationship perimeter, not an embedded B2B2C infrastructure.

## Why the venture's anchor pitch handles Layer D in KSA

Same structural argument as UAE Layer D, with a KSA-specific addition:

- KSA banks are **already loyalty-saturated at the headline-cashback level** (10% cashback on capped categories is table stakes). What they cannot deliver is **embedded, white-label, cross-acquirer cashback inside a non-bank enterprise anchor's app** — which is the venture's product.
- The KSA bank-anchor pitch is therefore: "*Your customer's Al Rajhi card already gives them Mokafaa. Your customer's Riyad card already gives them Hassad. What none of those give your customer is automatic cashback at the everyday merchant inside YOUR app, paid for by the merchant, with zero P&L cost to you.*"
- This is the **same gap** as Entertainer × HSBC validated in UAE [`entertainer.md` — VERIFIED]: the bank pays a third party for what the bank cannot build internally. KSA banks have not yet executed this trade publicly; the precedent is the gap.
