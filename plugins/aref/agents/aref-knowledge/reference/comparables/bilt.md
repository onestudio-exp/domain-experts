# Bilt Rewards (US)

## Tier classification: **Indirect**

Same outcome (consumer earns loyalty value on otherwise-unrewarded large recurring transactions) but a structurally different model from Amos:

- **Geography:** US only — different regulatory regime than CBUAE SVF/RPS, KSA SAMA, or Egypt CBE.
- **Funding mechanism:** Co-branded credit card with interchange-and-bank co-funding (Wells Fargo 2022–mid-2025; Cardless 2026→). Amos is **merchant-funded only** with no card co-funding.
- **Loop:** Open-loop point accrual. Amos uses closed-loop AED-denominated discount commitment.
- **Distribution:** Brand-led (Bilt as consumer-facing brand) layered inside anchor app. Amos is invisible white-label inside DAMAC Living, FAB, ADCB.
- **Anchor type:** Single-vertical (rent / mortgage) origin → expanding to neighborhood. Amos is multi-anchor from day one (real estate + bank + retail + airline + free-zone + cooperative).

The **outcome similarity** (rewarding involuntary monthly spend at scale) makes Bilt the canonical reference for "what good looks like at this category", which is why Amos's investor materials use Bilt's pre-anchor seed valuation as the apples-to-apples benchmark for Amos's $20M pre-money.

## Wells Fargo era (2022 — terminated mid-2025)

The original Bilt Card was issued by Wells Fargo from 2022. The economics were **structurally negative for Wells Fargo** because rent transactions in the US do not carry standard merchant interchange (landlord acceptance was the unique anchor) — making the bank the merchant-side payer for rewards.

| Fact | Source | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Wells Fargo reportedly losing ~$10M/month on the program (mid-2024) | PYMNTS 2024-06-16 | `[UNVERIFIED]` (single source) |
| Original contract end date: 2029 | Real Deal 2025-07-11 (citing WSJ) | `[VERIFIED]` (multiple downstream pickups) |
| Termination announced: July 10, 2025 — 5 years ahead of schedule | Reuters 2025-07-10, Real Deal 2025-07-11, Finimize 2025-07-10 | `[VERIFIED]` (3 independent sources) |
| Termination disclosed concurrent with $250M / $10.75B Series D | Reuters 2025-07-10 | `[VERIFIED]` |

**Lessons for Amos:** Co-brand card economics are sensitive to the underlying transaction's interchange permissibility. In Amos's MENA model, **merchants are direct payers** (MDR-funded), avoiding the structural mismatch that broke the Wells Fargo deal. CBUAE SVF + RPS classification of cashback as merchant-funded discount commitment (not bank-issued points) means Amos avoids the asymmetric loss exposure that ate Wells Fargo's program.

## Bilt Card 2.0 era (Jan 2026 — present)

January 14, 2026: Bilt launched **Bilt Card 2.0** — a suite of three credit cards powered by **Cardless** as digital-issuer technology platform `[VERIFIED]` (BusinessWire 2026-01-14, Crowdfund Insider 2026-01-18).

Key changes vs Wells Fargo era:

| Dimension | Wells Fargo era | Card 2.0 era |
|---|---|---|
| Eligible spend | Rent only | Rent **AND** mortgage (first time homeowners earn) |
| Issuer model | Bank-as-issuer | Digital-platform issuer (Cardless) |
| Card UX | Wells Fargo app + Bilt app | Native inside Bilt app (account, autopay, disputes, rewards) |
| Reward rate | Variable | 4% Bilt Cash on everyday spend; 10% intro APR for 12 months |
| Number of cards | 1 | 3 (tiered) |

**Strategic significance for Amos:** The Cardless model is **closer to Amos's embedded architecture** than the Wells Fargo bank-as-issuer model was. The card now lives **inside the loyalty app** rather than the bank's app — directionally validating Amos's "white-label inside the anchor experience" thesis.

**Reception risk:** NerdWallet flagged consumer confusion in the rollout `[VERIFIED]` (NerdWallet 2026-01-27) — the three-card structure and reward terms created comparison friction with Amex / Chase premium repricing of 2025. Worth tracking for Amos's tier-design lessons.

## Why Bilt is the canonical comparable for embedded loyalty

Bilt converted the largest involuntary monthly transaction Americans make — rent — into a loyalty earn event. Before Bilt, paying rent earned nothing. By signing major property managers (the anchors) to allow rent-on-card with no merchant fee, Bilt embedded itself into millions of apartment units and bootstrapped distribution at near-zero CAC.

The 30x+ valuation move from 2021 ($350M) to 2025 ($10.75B) was driven by:

1. The Wells Fargo card programme (anchor co-funding) — terminated 2025.
2. Network expansion from rent into local merchants ("neighborhood spend") — 40K+ locations by 2025.
3. Rent Day mechanics (gamified concentrated frequency).
4. Data-rich first-party transaction signal valued by partners.
5. Mortgage-eligible expansion in 2026 — opening a second largest-recurring-transaction category.

The 2026 transition to Cardless removes the bank co-funding dependency, shifting Bilt closer to a pure loyalty-platform monetization (interchange share + merchant fees) and reducing the structural fragility that ended the Wells Fargo deal.

### Why no Bilt-equivalent has emerged in UAE / KSA

The structural reasons map to the original Bilt-Wells-Fargo failure analysis in the body of this file:

1. **Card co-brand interchange economics.** UAE and KSA card-issuer interchange is structurally lower than US issuer interchange on the same transaction class — the unit economics of "merchant pays the rewards" are even thinner than what broke the Wells Fargo-Bilt deal.
2. **Rent-payment infrastructure differs.** UAE rent payments are dominated by Ejari-registered cheque-based or annual lump-sum mechanics; the recurring card-billed rent payment that Bilt depends on is not the dominant channel. Keyper / Rently / Takeem are addressing the upstream rent-payment infrastructure problem before a rewards mechanic on top can scale.
3. **Operator-led alternatives already exist.** Aldar Darna (UAE) and ROSHN's nascent Marketplace (KSA, `knowledge/reference/comparables/red-operator-loyalty.md`) demonstrate that REDs themselves are filling the housing-loyalty surface area on operator-by-operator basis, leaving little room for a category-infrastructure entrant unless distribution is via a non-RED anchor channel.
4. **Mortgage rewards require longer maturity.** Holo and Huspy are still scaling mortgage origination volumes in the GCC; layering loyalty on mortgage payments requires a denser mortgage-holder base than currently exists.

### Implication for the venture

- **The Bilt-equivalent category gap is real but does not directly translate to the venture's mechanic.** The venture is multi-anchor, multi-vertical, merchant-funded — not category-vertical-focused on rent or mortgage.
- However, **rent-payment cashback inside DAMAC LIVE (Amos) and inside any future RED-anchor (Sobha, ROSHN) is the closest active-MENA realization** of the Bilt thesis at the merchant-funded layer.
- **No stealth-stage Bilt-equivalent operator surfaced** in this 2026-05-06 sweep. If one emerges, it would most likely come via a RED-anchor partnership (Aldar / Emaar / ROSHN — operator-funded, not merchant-funded), not as an independent venture.
