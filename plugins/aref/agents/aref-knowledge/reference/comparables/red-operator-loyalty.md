# RED-Operator Loyalty Programmes — UAE & KSA

## Tier classification: **Indirect**

This file profiles the loyalty / community programmes operated by major real-estate developers (REDs) in UAE and KSA. The motivating question for the venture is: **does any RED operate a programme that a peer developer could license as embedded B2B2C cashback infrastructure?**

**Headline answer: No.** Every programme profiled below is operator-proprietary. RED-operator programmes do not exist as licensable B2B2C infrastructure for peer developers, because:

1. They are competitive products to other developers (Aldar will not license to Emaar; Sobha will not license to DAMAC).
2. They are funded by the operator's own marketing / partnership budgets, not by an underlying merchant-MDR mechanic that scales independent of the operator.
3. They are loosely scoped — facility booking, service charges, community-services discounts — and only thinly cross-vertical at the merchant cashback layer.

**Implication for the venture:** RED operators are **anchor candidates**, not competitors. The Amos × DAMAC LIVE deal [`knowledge/reference/case-studies/amos/damac-deal.md` — VERIFIED] is the canonical model — Amos is the **infrastructure** embedded inside DAMAC's app, not a competing programme. Any peer RED (Aldar, Emaar, Sobha, ROSHN, etc.) is a future anchor candidate for the venture, subject to category-exclusivity windows.

## Cross-cutting observations

1. **Two operating archetypes among UAE/KSA REDs:**
   - **Programmed (Aldar Darna, Emaar U by Emaar)** — already have proprietary loyalty layers; venture insertion requires augmentation, not replacement.
   - **App-without-loyalty (Sobha ONE, ROSHN Living)** — have resident super-apps but no embedded cashback / loyalty mechanic. **These are the cleanest anchor candidates for the venture.**
2. **Aldar's Darna is the closest UAE precedent to the venture's model class.** Darna links cards, earns on swipes across cross-vertical merchants, accepts Etihad Guest conversion, and accepts rent / school-fee redemption. It is **funded by Aldar's own balance-sheet** rather than merchants — confirming why "merchant-funded" is the differentiator, not "embedded loyalty."
3. **RED-loyalty programmes are not licensable to peer REDs.** Confirmed for all six entities profiled. The competitive question is therefore not "can a peer license [X]?" but "does the venture's mechanic compete favourably with [X]'s in-place programme at the anchor's procurement table?" — which is the Layer D framing in `05-competitive-analysis.md`.
4. **DAMAC LIVE / Amos is the canonical MENA precedent** for "merchant-funded cashback infrastructure embedded inside a RED's resident app." No other RED-operator has executed this pattern publicly as of 2026-05-06.

---

## Implications for the venture's anchor strategy

1. **Sobha and ROSHN are the highest-value un-programmed RED anchor candidates** in UAE and KSA respectively — they have resident-app footprints but no merchant-funded cashback mechanic.
2. **Aldar and Emaar are difficult anchor candidates** because their existing programmes (Darna, U by Emaar) create internal competition for the venture's surface area. Approaching them requires positioning as augmentation (cross-vertical merchant network outside operator-curated partners), not replacement.
3. **Diriyah, NEOM, and RSG are Phase 2+ candidates** — pre-resident-occupancy or niche second-home demographics.
4. **Per `knowledge/decisions/2026-05-05-precede-amos-strategy.md`:** DAMAC is locked to Amos in UAE; this file confirms that the next-best UAE-RED anchor candidate is **Sobha** (un-programmed app footprint, residential-community focus, alignable with the venture's mechanic without internal-programme conflict).
