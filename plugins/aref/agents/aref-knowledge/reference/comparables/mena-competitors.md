# MENA Loyalty Competitors: Competitive Positioning vs Amos

## Overview

The MENA loyalty market is large and growing but structurally fragmented. The UAE market alone was worth $325.2M in 2025, projected to reach $592.4M by 2030 (CAGR 12.3%, 2026-2030; historical CAGR 16.6%, 2021-2025). [vector: mena-competitors-noon-share-adnoc-2026-04-29.md, ingested 2026-04-29]. The broader Middle East market (GCC + wider region) is cited at $3.0B (2025) growing to $5.6B by 2030 (CAGR ~12.8%) — a different geographic scope that should not be conflated with the UAE-only figure. [vector: docs/AMOS_InvestorFactSheet_March2026.pdf, ingested 2026-04-29]

The dominant structural fact about every named MENA competitor is this: **they are all B2C consumer applications competing for downloads. Amos is infrastructure embedded inside enterprise apps.** Every competitor described below requires consumers to acquire and use a standalone app; Amos distributes zero-CAC through DAMAC Living, FAB/Payit, Union Coop, ADCB, and similar enterprise hosts. The architectural layer — not the reward mechanic — is the primary competitive moat.

The market is consolidating around ecosystem-led models. Standalone loyalty apps are already losing relevance as loyalty shifts toward embedded, transaction-linked rewards inside higher-frequency utility platforms (fuel, telecoms, real estate, banking). [vector: mena-competitors-noon-share-adnoc-2026-04-29.md, ingested 2026-04-29]

## Tier classifications

Per Aref's competitor schema (`Direct` = same merchant-funded cashback model + same MENA geography; `Indirect` = similar loyalty outcome via a different model; `Substitute` = different category competing for same wallet):

| Competitor | Tier | One-line reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| **e& Smiles** | **Indirect** | MENA ✓ but predominantly telecom-margin funded (hybrid with merchant vouchers); B2C standalone, not B2B2C. Geography matches Direct but funding model fails. |
| **Careem Plus / Pay** | **Indirect** | Subscription + platform-margin funded — Careem funds rewards from its own ride / delivery commissions, not merchants. Super-app embedded but not merchant-funded loyalty infra. |
| **stc Qitaf** | **Indirect** | Telco points programme; rewards funded from stc's telecom margin. Largest by member count (18M+) but a B2C consumer app, not embedded infrastructure. |
| **Majid Al Futtaim SHARE** | **Indirect** | Closed coalition within MAF properties (Carrefour, Mall of the Emirates, VOX); MAF balance-sheet funds points liability. No external licensing — enterprises outside MAF cannot access SHARE infrastructure. |
| **Noon Points** | **Indirect** | E-commerce platform loyalty, funded from Noon's e-comm margin; in-platform-only redemption. Different funnel position (online checkout) vs Amos's offline POS embedding. |
| **ADNOC Rewards** | **Indirect** | Vertical-specific fuel-and-convenience programme; ADNOC's own estate only; not externally licensable. |

**Key insight:** *Zero of the named MENA competitors operate a merchant-funded white-label B2B2C model.* Every one is a standalone B2C consumer app with rewards funded by the parent's own margin (telecom, e-commerce, retail, fuel). This is the structural moat the Amos thesis is built on — the architectural layer (embedded infrastructure) rather than the reward mechanic is the primary competitive differentiation. There are no MENA `Direct` comparables to Amos at present.

## Key Competitors

### 1. e& Smiles (formerly Etisalat Smiles) — UAE

**Description.** e& Smiles is the consumer lifestyle engagement programme of e& (formerly Etisalat), the UAE's largest telecom operator. It operates as a marketplace-style app rather than a traditional points programme, enabling members to access merchant offers, vouchers, and e& service rewards.

**User base.** 5M+ members (2024-2025 headline figure). Earlier sources cited 3M+ (2022-2023 vintage) and 2.2M app users; the 5M+ figure is the most recent authoritative number. e&'s broader subscriber base is 10M+. [vector: mena-competitors-etisalat-smiles-2026-04-29.md, ingested 2026-04-29]

**Merchant network.** 11,000+ restaurants, 500+ grocery stores and pharmacies, 35+ home services providers, 6,500 brand partners (recent sources). Older figures: 4,000+ partner merchants, 1,000+ participating brands. [vector: mena-competitors-etisalat-smiles-2026-04-29.md, ingested 2026-04-29]

**Revenue / reward funding model.** Hybrid: (a) e& subsidises rewards earned on telecom bills and recharges from telecom margin; (b) merchant-funded vouchers and discounts pushed through the Smiles marketplace; (c) interchange contribution via co-branded cards (e.g., CBD Smiles Visa Credit Card issued by CBD, not e&). Specific take-rate not publicly disclosed.

**Technology.** Etisalat launched a blockchain-powered Rewards Exchange in 2019 (Hyperledger Fabric on Microsoft Azure, built by Avanza Innovations) enabling real-time points exchange between partner programmes including GEMS Rewards, Adenok Rewards, Shukran (Landmark Group), and Emirates Islamic Bank.

**Regulatory profile.** Smiles operates under e&'s corporate umbrella with no separate CBUAE SVF licence. Points are non-cashable promotional instruments issued by e& directly. Co-branded card products issued by licensed banks.

**Weaknesses vs Amos.** Requires consumer app download (high CAC). Rewards partially e&-funded (balance sheet exposure). No white-label B2B2C enterprise licensing model. Merchant participation is a marketplace listing, not a performance-commission relationship.

### 2. Careem Super App / Careem Plus — UAE, MENA (owned by e&)

**Critical ownership note.** In December 2023, e& acquired a majority stake in Careem's Super App business for $400 million. As of 2025, e& controls both Smiles and the Careem Super App. Careem's ride-hailing business remains owned by Uber. The Smiles and Careem loyalty ecosystems are therefore both inside the e& group — they are complementary verticals of the same parent, not independent competitors. [vector: mena-competitors-careem-2026-04-29.md, ingested 2026-04-29]

**Description.** Careem operates as a super app spanning ride-hailing, food delivery, grocery, and payments (Careem Pay) across 13 countries. The loyalty layer has two components: (a) Careem Plus subscription and (b) Careem Pay cashback.

**Scale.** 50M+ registered users (MENA-wide, 2025). 2.5M captains (drivers). Revenue cited at ~$2.8B in 2025 across all verticals. [vector: mena-competitors-careem-2026-04-29.md, ingested 2026-04-29]

**Loyalty model — Careem Plus.** Paid subscription at AED 39/month: 10% instant cashback on Careem rides, 5% on HALA rides, free food delivery on participating restaurants. Initially Dubai-only. Careem funds this from platform commission revenue — it is not a merchant-funded model.

**Loyalty model — Careem Pay.** 1% cashback (up to AED 500) on international money transfers, credited to the Careem wallet (closed-loop). Separate from the subscription benefits.

**Merchant network.** IKEA, Amazon.ae, Zara, Papa John's, Pizza Hut, KFC, Landmark Group; thousands of F&B partners via Careem Food.

**Revenue / reward funding model.** Careem funds loyalty from its own P&L (ride commissions, delivery fees). Merchant participation is performance-marketing placement within the app — merchants pay for access to Careem's user base, not a commission-on-transaction infrastructure model.

**Regulatory profile.** Careem Pay holds UAE financial services/payments licence. e& ownership subjects the financial services layer to CBUAE oversight.

**Weaknesses vs Amos.** Rewards funded by Careem's own margin (P&L exposure). No B2B2C infrastructure licensing. User base is ride-hailing/delivery commuters — limited overlap with DAMAC residents, bank account holders, freezone tenants (Amos's primary cohort). Subscription model requires active consumer opt-in vs Amos's passive embedding.

**Future risk.** e& now controls both Smiles and Careem. If e& converges these into a unified embedded loyalty infrastructure offering (e.g., licensing a combined loyalty layer to enterprises), it would be a material competitive threat. No evidence of this to date.

### 3. stc Qitaf (نقاط / nuqat) — KSA

**Naming note.** "stc nqat" or "نقاط stc" is the Arabic colloquial reference to stc's points programme. The formal brand is **Qitaf** (قطاف). These terms are synonymous.

**Description.** Qitaf is Saudi Telecom Company's consumer loyalty programme — the largest loyalty programme in the Middle East by member count. Open to stc subscribers and (via Qitaf Non-stc) customers of other Saudi operators.

**User base.** From stc investor relations:
- H1 2025: 18.43 million members (vs 16.04M in H1 2024; +2.4M year-on-year)
- End of 2024: 18.03 million members
- Q1 2025: 17.90 million members
[vector: mena-competitors-stc-qitaf-2026-04-29.md, ingested 2026-04-29; source: stc Earnings Presentations Q1 2025 and FY 2024]

**Merchant / partner network.** 34+ partner companies, 150+ local and international brands, 3,300+ partner outlets across KSA and internationally. Redemption: 5 Qitaf points = 1 SAR.

**Revenue / reward funding model.** Points earned primarily on stc telecom spend — stc subsidises from telecom margin. Redemption side involves merchant-funded vouchers/discounts via the partner catalogue. Qitaf Business variant for SME customers. Specific commission structure not publicly disclosed.

**B2B / white-label.** No evidence of white-label B2B2C enterprise licensing. Qitaf is a wholly owned stc consumer programme.

**Regulatory profile.** Operates under telecom regulatory umbrella; points are non-cashable promotional instruments. No SAMA SVF licence required.

**Geographic relevance.** KSA-focused. Amos plans KSA market entry in 2027. Qitaf will be the ambient loyalty brand Amos encounters across KSA enterprise clients and their consumer bases.

**Weaknesses vs Amos.** Telecom-margin subsidised (balance sheet exposure at scale). No enterprise B2B2C licensing. Primarily telecoms-linked earn — does not cover the real estate, banking, retail verticals Amos targets. KSA-only, no UAE presence.

**Potential synergy / risk.** Amos investor deck notes Saudi National Bank as a direct relationship target. stc Pay (stc's financial services arm) and Qitaf could theoretically converge into a bank-linked embedded loyalty play, but no evidence of this architecture exists as of April 2026.

### 4. Majid Al Futtaim SHARE — UAE, MENA

**Description.** SHARE is the coalition loyalty programme spanning Carrefour, VOX Cinemas, Mall of the Emirates, Ski Dubai, and other Majid Al Futtaim (MAF) assets. Described as "the UAE's fastest growing loyalty programme" with AI-driven personalisation.

**User base.** 10M+ SHARE members (cited in MAF's Ma'an SME programme announcement). [vector: mena-competitors-noon-share-adnoc-2026-04-29.md, ingested 2026-04-29]

**Revenue model.** MAF-subsidised points (earned within MAF venues, funded by retail margin) plus merchant-funded offers within MAF's retail ecosystem. Co-branded cards: ADIB-MAF Sharia-compliant SHARE Covered Cards.

**B2B element (Ma'an).** The Ma'an programme gives UAE SMEs placement access to 10M+ SHARE members via MAF retail channels. This is merchant onboarding into MAF's consumer programme — not an enterprise infrastructure licensing model.

**Weaknesses vs Amos.** Coalition programme requiring membership app download. Earn and redemption concentrated within MAF-owned properties (Carrefour, VOX, MAF malls) — limited cross-sector utility. MAF funds the points liability. Not a B2B2C white-label infrastructure.

### 5. Noon Points — UAE, KSA, Egypt

**Description.** Noon Points is e-commerce embedded loyalty: points earned on every Noon platform purchase, redeemable for discounts, gifts, and promotional benefits. Noon VIP is an upgraded tier with AI-personalised discounts and early deal access.

**User base.** Not publicly disclosed. Noon overall has tens of millions of registered users across UAE, KSA, Egypt.

**Revenue model.** Noon-funded (customer acquisition cost via discounts). Not a merchant-funded infrastructure model. Noon absorbs the loyalty cost as a retention mechanism within its e-commerce platform.

**Co-branded partnership.** Mashreq and noon.com launched a VIP savings account (co-branded banking product).

**Weaknesses vs Amos.** E-commerce-only earn (not cross-sector). Noon funds the loyalty from its own P&L. No B2B2C enterprise infrastructure. Limited to Noon ecosystem for earn and redemption.

---

### 6. ADNOC Distribution Rewards — UAE

**Description.** Fuel and convenience retail loyalty; one of the UAE's strongest everyday-use programmes by transaction frequency. Earn on fuel, convenience store (ADNOC Oasis), and car services at ADNOC stations.

**Strength.** High-frequency touchpoint (daily petrol fill-up) drives engagement rates that most loyalty programmes cannot match in the UAE.

**Revenue model.** ADNOC-subsidised (funded from fuel/retail margin) plus partner merchant redemption.

**Weaknesses vs Amos.** Vertically contained (fuel/retail). ADNOC funds the points liability. No B2B2C infrastructure licensing. Channel does not reach real estate, banking, or freezone enterprise hosts.

## Amos Differentiation

### vs e& Smiles (and Careem as e& subsidiary)
- e& funds a portion of Smiles rewards from telecom margin; Amos externalises 100% of reward cost to merchants. The enterprise host carries zero liability.
- Smiles is a consumer app competing for downloads; Amos deploys inside DAMAC Living, FAB, Union Coop — reaching residents in their existing digital environment.
- e&'s ownership of both Smiles and Careem Super App creates a potential future convergence risk if e& builds enterprise loyalty licensing infrastructure. As of April 2026 there is no evidence of this.
- CCO Sanjit Gill joined Amos from Collinson Group (UAE Regional MD) — directly competitive to the type of enterprise loyalty relationships e& would need to build.

### vs stc Qitaf
- Amos KSA entry (planned 2027) will not directly compete with Qitaf for consumer mindshare; it will offer KSA enterprise clients (banks, developers, retailers) what Qitaf cannot: white-label embedded loyalty with zero balance-sheet liability funded entirely by merchants.
- Qitaf's 18M+ members represent market familiarity with earn-on-telecoms loyalty — a consumer education advantage but not a structural barrier to an enterprise-licensing B2B2C model.
- Saudi National Bank is in Amos's direct relationship pipeline, which would create a Qitaf-adjacent distribution foothold in KSA.

### vs Majid Al Futtaim SHARE
- SHARE earns within MAF properties; Amos is sector-agnostic (real estate, banking, retail, travel). Enterprises outside MAF cannot access SHARE's infrastructure.
- MAF funds the points liability; Amos inverts this — the enterprise receives a revenue share rather than a cost centre.
- SHARE's 10M members create strong coalition brand recognition in UAE, but no enterprise can license SHARE infrastructure for their own customer base.

### vs Noon Points
- Noon's loyalty is contained within its e-commerce platform; Amos operates across dining, entertainment, travel, retail (PrioHub 900+ attractions + 170+ direct merchants).
- Noon funds loyalty from its own margin; Amos's merchants fund cashback from their own customer acquisition budgets, making Amos margin-positive from day one.

### vs ADNOC Rewards
- ADNOC Rewards is vertically specialised (fuel/convenience); Amos is cross-vertical.
- Different enterprise host profile: ADNOC Rewards is a proprietary programme for ADNOC's own estate; Amos is an infrastructure layer deployable inside any enterprise app.
