# NPS — Net Promoter Score

## Summary

"On a scale of 0–10, how likely are you to recommend this to a colleague or friend?" Score = % promoters (9-10) − % detractors (0-6). NPS is a useful directional sentiment indicator, weak as a churn predictor on its own.

## Strengths

- Single, easily benchmarked number across companies and time.
- Captures emotional dimension that transactional metrics miss.
- Forces the verbatim follow-up ("why did you give that score?") which is where the actual insight lives.

## Weaknesses

- Cultural bias. Middle Eastern respondents systematically score lower than US for the same satisfaction level.
- Sampling bias. Promoters disproportionately respond.
- No causal mapping to behaviour without joining to transaction data.
- Easily gamed when used as an OKR.

## How to use it well in loyalty

1. **Anchor-segmented NPS** — score by anchor (DAMAC residents vs FAB Bank customers), not blended.
2. **Verbatim coding** — categorise free-text follow-ups quarterly; the codes are the actual KPI.
3. **Behavioural overlay** — join NPS to next-90-day transaction count. Detractors who keep transacting are Stockholm-syndrome users; promoters who stop are coasting.
4. **Internal benchmark, not external** — comparing across markets is noise. Compare cohort-over-cohort.

## NPS for the three loyalty audiences

- **End user NPS** — what consumer surveys traditionally measure.
- **Anchor NPS** — survey the anchor's product / loyalty / digital teams quarterly. Their NPS predicts renewal far better than user NPS.
- **Merchant NPS** — measure post-onboarding and at month 3. Drives merchant churn risk.
