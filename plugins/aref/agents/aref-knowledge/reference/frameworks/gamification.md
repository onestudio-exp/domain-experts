# Gamification

## Summary

Game mechanics — points, tiers, badges, streaks, leaderboards, unlocks, surprise rewards — applied to loyalty programs to lift engagement frequency and emotional connection. Used well, gamification is a frequency multiplier; used poorly, it is theatre that adds liability without behaviour change.

## The four mechanic families

1. **Progress** — visible accumulation toward a goal (points-to-next-tier, milestone bars). Best for monthly active engagement.
2. **Status** — public or private rank that confers benefits (Bronze→Platinum). Best for retention and willingness-to-pay.
3. **Variability** — surprise rewards, scratch cards, double-cashback days. Best for short-term frequency lift.
4. **Social** — leaderboards, referrals, gifting. Best for acquisition, dangerous for loyalty (creates losers).

## Design principles

- **Tie every mechanic to a behaviour you actually want.** A streak that drives daily check-ins on an app the user doesn't need to open daily is friction, not engagement.
- **Make the reward genuinely valuable.** A badge with no economic substance is decoration; in MENA markets in particular, consumers are direct about valuing money rewards over symbolic ones.
- **Avoid losers.** Public leaderboards in a loyalty program churn the bottom 80% who can't compete with whales.
- **Decay rules must be visible.** Silent point expiry destroys trust and is regulatorily exposed in some jurisdictions.

## Bilt's playbook

Bilt's "Rent Day" (the 1st of each month) doubles points on dining and Lyft for 24h. This is variability + progress combined: it concentrates engagement on a date users already remember (rent day) and produces a measurable monthly frequency spike. Worth studying as the cleanest gamified reward day in modern loyalty.

## Anti-patterns

- Tier downgrade via silent decay → churn.
- Badges with no economic substance.
- Leaderboards that surface real customer identities.
- Streaks longer than the user's natural cadence (a "30-day streak" on grocery shopping shames non-daily shoppers).
