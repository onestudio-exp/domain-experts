# MENA SME Merchant Economics

## Why this primer exists

Killer Assumption #1 in the venture's discovery cluster is: **"MENA SME merchants are willing to commit ≥12% MDR-share for performance customer access via embedded cashback infrastructure."** This file aggregates the available evidence on that assumption from public 2024-2026 sources — survey data, comparable mechanics, and category benchmarks.

**Headline finding:** The 12%+ MDR-share commitment is **not directly attested** in any public survey or report. However, the closest comparable mechanics (Talabat 15-30% commission, Entertainer BOGO with implicit 50% loss-leader discount) confirm MENA SME merchants **do** commit double-digit margin shares for performance customer access — at rates **above** the venture's 12% floor. The 12% floor is therefore positioned at the low end of the comparable-mechanic range, which strengthens the willingness-to-fund thesis at face value.

## Section 2 — Comparable mechanics: implicit MDR-share willingness

This is where the strongest evidence sits. The venture's 12% MDR-share floor must be assessed against what MENA SMEs **already pay** for performance customer access via comparable mechanics.

### Talabat commission rates (food delivery — strongest single benchmark)

| Finding | Confidence | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Talabat commission ranges **15% to 30%** of order value for restaurants | `[VERIFIED]` | blog.menuviel.com (2025 guide); reconcileos.com (UAE 2026 guide); enatega.com |
| Commission tier varies by restaurant tier, location, order volume, service level (self-delivery vs Talabat-delivered) | `[VERIFIED]` | Multiple sources |
| Restaurants exclusive to Talabat get a 5-10% commission reduction | `[VERIFIED]` | blog.menuviel.com |
| Dubai Restaurants Group members receive discounted Talabat fees + commission | `[VERIFIED]` | caterermiddleeast.com |
| 60%+ of UAE restaurants use Talabat as primary delivery platform | `[UNVERIFIED]` (single source) | reconcileos.com |
| Talabat MAU reached 7.5M in Q3 2025, +23% YoY | `[UNVERIFIED]` (single source) | enatega.com |
| Restaurants in MENA describe Talabat commission rates as "costly" — Khaleej Times article reports operator pushback | `[VERIFIED]` (Khaleej Times is credible publisher) | khaleejtimes.com |
| Jordanian restaurants pay ~30% to Talabat | `[UNVERIFIED]` (single source, blog) | nexaratech.io |

**Implication for the venture's 12% MDR-share floor:** The Talabat rate range (15-30%) is **above** the venture's 12% floor. UAE / KSA dining merchants demonstrably accept this rate range — though with documented dissatisfaction. The venture's pitch needs to position 12% MDR-share against the Talabat baseline as a **lower-friction alternative for the same outcome (incremental customer acquisition)**, with two structural advantages:
1. No platform disintermediation of the customer relationship.
2. Cashback at POS, not order-time platform commission — preserving direct customer relationship.

The framing for the venture is: "*Entertainer takes 50% of the qualifying transaction value off the merchant's margin. We take 12% of MDR. Same goal — performance customer access — at one-quarter the merchant cost.*"

## Section 4 — What this evidence does NOT confirm

The evidence in this primer **does not directly confirm**:
- That the venture-specific 12% MDR-share commit is the median accepted rate across UAE/KSA SMEs (only that comparable mechanics demand higher rates).
- That MDR-share commitment is preferred over fixed-fee placement (Entertainer mechanic) by SMEs as a class.
- That SMEs will sign multi-year category-exclusivity terms at the 12% rate (the Amos benchmark is per-merchant; cross-merchant exclusivity is a separate question).
- That non-dining categories accept 12% at the same rate as dining accepts 15-30% (Talabat — likely no for thin-margin categories).

These remain `[NEEDS-RESEARCH]` and are appropriately flagged in the venture's Killer Assumption #1 framing.
