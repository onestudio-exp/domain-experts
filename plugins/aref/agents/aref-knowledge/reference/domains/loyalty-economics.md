# Loyalty Program Unit Economics

## Summary

Loyalty programs convert engagement into incremental margin. The economics depend on who funds the reward, how breakage is treated, and whether the program is closed- or open-loop.

## The four funding models

1. **Operator-funded** — the loyalty operator pays for the reward from its own margin. Pure marketing expense; ROI = incremental spend × take rate vs reward cost.
2. **Merchant-funded** — merchants fund the discount in exchange for incremental traffic. The Amos model. Operator earns a take of the merchant's incremental margin.
3. **Co-funded (banking partnership)** — issuer bank funds part of the cashback in exchange for card spend. Bilt + Wells Fargo.
4. **Customer-funded subscription** — customer pays a fee for access to discounts. The Entertainer's model.

## Where each model wins

- **Operator-funded:** brand-building or customer acquisition campaigns; not a sustainable core program.
- **Merchant-funded:** large network of merchants with structurally high margin (F&B, leisure, services). Scales by adding supply.
- **Co-funded:** when a bank partner has a vested interest in card spend uplift. Highest LTV per user but slowest to set up.
- **Subscription:** when discounts are deep and the user thinks of the membership as a product.

## Take-rate benchmarks (industry observed)

| Category | Merchant margin | Operator take |
|---|---|---|
| F&B (casual dining) | 12–18% | 1–3% of TPV (10–20% of merchant margin) |
| Travel & attractions | 8–15% | 1–2% of TPV |
| Electronics retail | 4–8% | 0.4–0.8% of TPV |
| Wellness / fitness | 15–25% | 1.5–3% of TPV |
| Groceries (non-promotional) | 1–3% | 0.1–0.3% of TPV |

These are observed working ranges; real terms vary by negotiation power.

## Working capital

In a discount-commitment model the operator typically funds cashback to the user immediately while waiting for merchant settlement (T+30 in many markets). At scale this is meaningful working capital exposure; some operators factor merchant receivables to manage it.

## Why merchant-funded plus enterprise-anchored is structurally interesting

- Zero CAC for end users.
- Take rate scales with TPV without per-user cost.
- AI cost-to-serve makes merchant ops nearly fixed-cost.
- Regulatory clearance once (per market) is reusable.
