# Amos — Regulatory Clearance

## Summary

In early 2026, Amos secured **formal written confirmation from Network International** (NI) — its principal payment gateway partner and a UAE Central Bank (CBUAE)-licensed payment service provider — that Amos's operating model is fully compliant with UAE Central Bank regulations governing **Stored Value Facilities** and **Retail Payment Services**.

This was not a routine compliance exercise. Amos's B2B2C cashback model is genuinely novel; no provider in the UAE had previously presented this structure. NI's compliance and legal teams, including external regulatory counsel, analysed it from first principles across multiple rounds of detailed submission.

## The conclusion

> "AMOS does not require a payment licence to operate. It orchestrates transactions through NI's licensed infrastructure; its cashback mechanism is a contractual discount commitment funded from AMOS's own margin, not a stored value instrument. Network International confirmed this position in writing."  
> — *AMOS Stakeholder Update, March 2026*

## Why the approval has structural significance

1. **Enterprise clients** — DAMAC, ADCB, and FAB Bank specifically — require documented regulatory assurance before embedding Amos into their own applications. The NI confirmation provides exactly that.
2. **Reusability** — the compliance framework validated here applies across every subsequent enterprise deployment. Each new client does not restart the regulatory review process.
3. **Regional expansion** — the analysis provides a strong foundation for KSA, Egypt, and other target-market regulator discussions.
4. **Investor protection** — a platform that cannot process payments has no business. This approval removed what was, for a period, an existential operational risk.

## What the approval is NOT

- It is **not a payment licence** (Amos does not need one).
- It is **not a CBUAE direct ruling** — it is NI's written compliance position, backed by external regulatory counsel.
- It is **not unconditional** — material changes to the model (e.g., adding a user-controlled wallet balance) could invalidate it. See [`regulatory/cbuae-svf.md`](../../regulatory/cbuae-svf.md).

## Practical use of the approval

- Cited in MSA risk-and-compliance schedules for DAMAC, FAB Bank, ADCB.
- Reusable as legal precedent in KSA / Egypt regulator engagement.
- A barrier-to-entry for new competitors who would need to repeat months of original legal analysis.
