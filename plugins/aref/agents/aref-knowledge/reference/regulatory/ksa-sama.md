# KSA — SAMA Payment Service Provider Regulations

## Summary

The Saudi Central Bank (SAMA) regulates Payment Service Providers under the Payment Services Provider Regulations (issued 2020 and updated subsequently). Any entity initiating, processing, or holding payment value in Saudi Arabia falls under this framework.

For loyalty / cashback operators expanding into KSA, the principles mirror the UAE structure:

- Holding stored value → licence required.
- Discount commitment funded from operator margin via a licensed PSP → licence not required.
- However: SAMA's interpretation has historically been more conservative than CBUAE's; what gets cleared in UAE may need a fresh ruling in KSA.

## SAMA Sandbox

SAMA operates a regulatory sandbox that has graduated several payments fintechs. A sandbox application is the lowest-friction path for novel B2B2C cashback models to test in-market without immediate full licensure.

## Key practical differences from UAE

- KSA Open Banking Framework (Phase 1 live) creates more scope for account-to-account loyalty flows but also more regulatory surface.
- Localisation of data is enforced; offshore hosting of KSA customer data attracts heightened scrutiny.
- Arabic-language consumer disclosures are explicitly required in some product contexts.

## Implications for an Amos-style expansion

1. Begin with a sandbox application or written no-action letter — not a direct licence.
2. Replicate the NI / CBUAE letter pattern with a SAMA-licensed PSP partner.
3. Plan for in-region hosting from day one (matches the FAB Bank UAE-hosting precedent).
