# Egypt — Central Bank of Egypt Payment Systems Law

## Summary

Egypt's payment regulation centres on the Central Bank Law No. 194 of 2020 and earlier electronic-payment instruments. The CBE has progressively brought e-money issuers, payment agents, and electronic-payment service providers under licensure.

For loyalty / cashback platforms entering Egypt:

- The market is large and underserved (loyalty is largely retail-issued, fragmented).
- Currency volatility is a structural concern — cashback denominated in EGP can lose meaningful value during a settlement window.
- Licensing path is more complex than UAE / KSA; partnership with an existing licensed e-payment institution is the practical entry route.

## Key regulatory features

- E-money issuance is licensed; only banks and specific e-money issuers may provide it.
- Card-linked offer providers operate in a less explicitly regulated space; CBE has issued circulars expanding scope.
- Cross-border value transfer is more restricted than in GCC.

## Practical implications

1. Egypt entry should follow KSA, not precede it — let the regulatory model harden first.
2. Currency hedging is part of the unit economics; cashback denominated in EGP is materially different from AED.
3. Egyptian merchants accept significantly lower take rates; expect compressed margins vs UAE.
