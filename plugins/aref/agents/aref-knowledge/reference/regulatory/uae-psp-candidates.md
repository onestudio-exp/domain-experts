# CBUAE-Licensed PSP Candidates Outside Network International

## Why this primer exists

Per `knowledge/reference/case-studies/amos/regulatory-clearance.md` — VERIFIED: Amos's CBUAE PSP-route written clearance for closed-loop AED-denominated discount commitment runs **through Network International (NI)**. The venture seeking to precede Amos in unlocked categories (per the 2026-05-05 decision) needs a non-NI PSP partner — both for licensing-route diversification and for differentiated commercial terms.

This file profiles the credible alternative CBUAE-licensed PSP / acquirer candidates as of 2026-05-06.

**Headline finding:** Three CBUAE-licensed candidates (Magnati, Checkout.com UAE, PayTabs UAE-presence) and three with regulatory-status caveats (Telr, Stripe UAE, Geidea KSA-primary) constitute the realistic universe. **No public commentary from any of the six on closed-loop discount-commitment treatment in MENA was surfaced** — this is a structural gap requiring direct engagement, not desk research.

---

## CBUAE PSP regulatory framework — quick context

The CBUAE Retail Payment Services and Card Schemes Regulation (RPSCS) — issued 2021, in effect — established a nine-category licensing regime [`[VERIFIED]` — rulebook.centralbank.ae]:

1. Payment Account Issuance Services
2. Payment Instrument Issuance Services
3. Merchant Acquiring Services
4. Payment Aggregation Services
5. Domestic Fund Transfer Services
6. Cross-border Fund Transfer Services
7. Payment Token Services
8. Payment Initiation Services
9. Payment Account Information Services

Mainland UAE PSPs must obtain CBUAE licensing. Minimum capital is AED 2M with AML, PCI DSS, 3D Secure, and AI-based fraud-detection requirements. A separate Stored Value Facility (SVF) regulation governs prepaid / stored-value products and is the relevant track for the venture's closed-loop cashback construct (cross-reference `knowledge/reference/regulatory/cbuae-svf.md`).

**Critical regulatory framing for the venture:** The Amos pattern uses a PSP-route written clearance for closed-loop discount commitment (not SVF licensure directly). A successor venture would either (a) replicate the PSP-route via a different licensed PSP (this file's question), or (b) pursue SVF licensure directly (longer track, not the operational-day-1 path). This file addresses (a).

## Cross-cutting observations

1. **No PSP candidate has published commentary on closed-loop discount-commitment treatment.** This is structurally expected — closed-loop discount commitment is a niche regulatory construct, and PSP commercial teams do not publish positions on niche regulatory constructs absent a specific deal trigger. **Direct engagement with each candidate's regulatory and commercial teams is the only reliable path** to assess willingness.
2. **Magnati + Geidea is the strongest UAE+KSA dual-coverage hypothesis** — both are MENA-headquartered acquirers with regulatory primacy in their respective markets and bank-level credibility. PayTabs is the leading single-vendor candidate for both markets if dual-coverage is operationally preferred over best-of-breed-per-market.
3. **Checkout.com is the strongest "global player in MENA" candidate** — first CBUAE-licensed global acquirer; Entertainer-relationship gives a warm-intro path; product surface is closed-loop-adjacent though not closed-loop-native.
4. **Stripe is the weakest fit for the closed-loop construct** despite being a strong UAE-payment-flow provider. Should not be the primary regulatory-route partner.
5. **Telr is a credible secondary candidate** — 20+ years UAE operational history, but smaller than Magnati / Checkout.com and likely to be more transactional than infrastructure-partnership-oriented.

## Implication for the venture

- **Primary UAE candidate to engage in priority order:** Magnati → Checkout.com → PayTabs → Telr.
- **Primary KSA candidate to engage:** Geidea (as SAMA-licensed acquirer with bank-tier credibility), with PayTabs as secondary.
- **Stripe** is not the primary regulatory-route partner; engage only for adjacent payment-flow needs.
- **Engagement question to all candidates:** Does the candidate have prior experience structuring closed-loop AED-denominated (UAE) or SAR-denominated (KSA) discount commitment as a payment-flow extension? If no precedent, can the candidate work with the venture and CBUAE / SAMA to obtain a written-clearance opinion analogous to the Amos × Network International precedent?

The path to clarity is not desk research — it is qualified outbound engagement with the regulatory and commercial teams of the named candidates. Each engagement should produce either a written opinion or a documented decline, both of which de-risk the regulatory roadmap.
